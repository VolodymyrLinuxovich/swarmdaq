\systemname{} is a technical system for adaptive AI-agent orchestration in which agents are treated as market participants rather than static workers in a fixed pipeline. Given a user mission, the system decomposes work into tasks, asks candidate agents to bid with expected quality, confidence, cost, latency, and risk estimates, and selects a swarm through a mathematical \marketmaker{} that combines skill matching, UCB1 exploration, Bayesian reputation, bid utility, Elo-style pairwise rating, graph trust, collaboration history, cost, latency, and uncertainty. Selected agents execute tasks through Gemini-backed reasoning when credentials are available, with deterministic fallback behavior for reliable demonstration. Each mission is traced through \weave{} integration points and a local trace store; outputs are evaluated across quality, factuality, usefulness, specificity, actionability, and collaboration. Reputation is then persisted through Upstash Redis when configured, or an in-memory store otherwise. The resulting loop turns evaluation into memory: agents that hallucinate lose reputation, reliable collaborators gain trust, and future swarms are selected with updated evidence. This paper presents \systemname{} as a prototype performance exchange for self-improving AI agent swarms, describes its implemented Next.js and TypeScript architecture, formalizes its routing equations, and reports its internal repeated-run demo as illustrative evidence of adaptive selection rather than as an external benchmark claim.
